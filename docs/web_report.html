A/B Test Executive Report • Experiment Window: Feb 01 – Feb 28, 2026

New Customer experience

Assessing Primary Metric (Revenue per assigned customer) & Guardrail metrics

📌 Executive Summary & Critical Findings

While the primary metric RAC (Revenue per Assigned Customer) showed an observed relative uplift of +6.37% (exceeding the target 2.0% threshold), the test experience failed both mandatory guardrail criteria with significant drops in Conversion Rate (-3.23%) and Orders per Assigned Customer (-5.62%).

⚠️
Data Quality & Pipeline Alert: The sample size difference between groups is statistically significant, exhibiting a clear assignment imbalance. The origin of the group creation pipeline must be thoroughly investigated to determine where this sample mismatch originated. Furthermore, there is a distinct difference in customer type concentration, with an over-representation of High Historical Value customers in the Test group (24.90% in Test vs 20.21% in Control), which artificially inflates the average revenue per customer.

🎯 Key Questions & Executive Conclusions

Structured synthesis evaluating business performance, statistical validity, and strategic rollout decision

1

Did the Test experience achieve its business objective?

The primary metric RAC showed an observed relative uplift of +6.37%, exceeding the +2.0% target. However, this result is flagged with a WARNING because severe Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) and customer selection bias mean the revenue gain cannot be reliably attributed to the test experience.

Conditionally Achieved / High Risk
2

What is the estimated impact on primary KPI and guardrails?

Evaluated at a 5% significance level (α = 0.05):

  • • RAC (Primary): +6.37% (€51.71 Test vs €48.61 Control, p = 0.00718)
  • • Conversion Rate (Guardrail 1): -3.23% (54.00% Test vs 55.81% Control, p = 1.0000)
  • • OPAC (Guardrail 2): -5.62% (0.8388 Test vs 0.8887 Control, p = 1.0000)
Failed Both Guardrails
3

How confident are you in the result?

Low Confidence. Statistical integrity is severely compromised by Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), and we currently do not know the root cause or origin of this assignment imbalance in the group allocation. Additionally, the historical customer segment distortion (24.90% High Value in Test vs 20.21% in Control) artificially skews metrics.

Low Confidence
4

Data-quality or experiment-design considerations?

Essential data cleansing was completed: 160 exact duplicate rows and 90 cross-assigned customers (who received both variants) were excluded. However, an assignment pipeline anomaly generated a significant SRM and segment bias, invalidating causal conclusions from this test run.

Data Integrity Flags
5

Would you recommend rolling out the new experience? Explain your reasoning and propose next steps.

Executive Verdict: NOT ROLLOUT. Although the primary metric achieved the +2% uplift business target (+6.37%), the test experience failed both guardrail metrics by violating the 1% non-inferiority threshold (Conversion Rate dropped -3.23% and Orders per Customer dropped -5.62%). Furthermore, the positive primary metric is compromised by sample ratio mismatch and selection bias.

Proposed Next Steps:
  1. Investigate the traffic allocation pipeline to uncover the root cause of the assignment imbalance.
  2. Fix bucket allocation and introduce stratified randomization across customer historical value tiers.
  3. Re-run the A/B test with a verified, balanced sample before making product rollout decisions.
Final Recommendation: NOT ROLLOUT
